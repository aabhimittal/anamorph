How to use and read this page

Use it

  1. Pick a clip. Each is a small tile (96–128 nm) of a typical High-NA pattern: line/space, tip-to-tip (two line ends facing across a gap), or a via array.
  2. Pick a mask. "Target as drawn" sends the design straight to the mask with no correction. The two ILT masks were optimized by the engine: one for edge placement only, one also for stochastic failure.
  3. Slide the dose (85–115%) to expose harder or softer and watch the printed edges move.
  4. Paint on the mask. Drag with the Open or Absorber brush; pick 1, 2 or 4 nm. The wafer updates as you draw. Reset mask restores the selected mask.
  5. Tick "reticle scale" to see the same mask as it sits on the reticle: stretched twice as tall as wide, because High-NA demagnifies 4× in x and 8× in y.
  6. Draw your own pattern. Choose "Draw your own" (a 128 nm tile with a starter drawing), "Clear mask" for a blank one, then drag with the Rectangle tool or paint with the Brush. 1 pixel is 1 nm on the wafer. Your drawing is the target for both EUV and nanoimprint.

Read it

Mask panel
Light pixels reflect EUV light (open multilayer mirror), dark pixels absorb it. The teal dashed outline is the pattern you want on the wafer.
Wafer panel
The color is the light dose the resist received after acid blur (dark = little, pale = a lot). Resist prints where it exceeds the threshold; the white line is that printed edge. A good mask puts the white line on the teal dashed target.
Gauges
Short lines and dots at the places a pattern can fail: a break (a line pinches off), a bridge (two features merge) or a missing via. Color is the engine's failure probability, P_fail, per gauge.
Worst P_fail
The riskiest gauge. 10−9 means about one failure per billion copies of that feature, which is the scale a chip with billions of vias needs. Higher (closer to 0) is worse.
Error marks
Red pixels printed where nothing should be; blue pixels should have printed but did not. Numbered dots point to the errors listed under "EUV lithography vs. nanoimprint".
Nanoimprint panel
The same target made by pressing a 1× template into resist. No optics, so no blur: only about 1 nm of corner rounding from resist flow. Its weak point is particles, which you can drop in with the button below the panels.
Other metrics
Printed vs target counts wrong pixels at the current dose. PV band is the area that changes across ±3% dose and 20 nm defocus (smaller is more robust). MRC counts mask features too small to manufacture. Model check confirms this page matches the Python engine.

Try this

  • Shape vs. survival. On Tip-to-tip, step through the three masks. Edge-placement ILT matches the target best, yet its worst gauge is 10−5.3, worse than the uncorrected target (10−9.2). The stochastic-aware mask reaches 10−14.8 by giving up some shape fidelity: more wrong pixels, far fewer failures.
  • Why masks need correction. On Line/space, choose "Target as drawn": the printed lines miss the target and the worst gauge sits at 10−4.4. Switch to either ILT mask and the edges snap onto the target.
  • Dose sensitivity. On Vias, drag the dose from 85% to 115% and watch the openings grow. P_fail stays at its 100%-dose value; only the image is recomputed.
  • Below the resolution limit. In "Draw your own", the 6 nm line on the right vanishes in EUV (the lens cannot resolve features under about 6–7 nm) but imprints in NIL. EUV also rounds every corner and pulls the L's open end back about 3 nm; EUV gets roughly 1,000 nm² wrong against about 30 nm² for NIL. Then draw a rectangle a few nm from another shape and watch EUV bridge them.
  • Blur vs. particles. On Tip-to-tip, compare the yield rows for each mask, then slide the NIL defect density. The edge-placement mask fails a 1 cm² area almost surely; the stochastic-aware mask beats NIL until NIL gets below about 10−4 particles per cm².
  • Break it yourself. Paint absorber across one tip-to-tip line end, or open a pixel in a gap, and watch where the printed edge moves. Gauges turn grey because P_fail needs the engine.

Same pattern, two ways to make it

EUV lithography vs. nanoimprint

Nanoimprint (NIL) presses a 1× quartz template into liquid resist and cures it with UV light: a stamp, not a projector. It copies shapes almost exactly, so it needs no mask correction, but a particle trapped under the template stops the pattern forming over a micrometer-scale area. EUV printing errors

    Nanoimprint errors

      How to read this: errors are found by comparing each printed shape with the shape it should be. A bridge is two features printed as one, a break one feature printed in pieces, missing a feature that did not print, extra a print where nothing was drawn. Shortened lines, width and size errors are measured on each feature's outline and reported from 2 nm up. Random failures are scaled to a 1 cm² area filled with this pattern; yield is the chance that area has none (Poisson). EUV random failures come from photon shot noise and are computed by the engine for the preset masks only. NIL failures come from particles: each particle wipes out a region about 1 µm across, so it counts as one failure whatever the pattern.

      The NIL model is illustrative, not calibrated to a tool. It covers 1× copying with about 1 nm of corner rounding, and Poisson particle defects at the density you set. It leaves out resist non-fill, pull-out on separation, residual-layer variation, template wear and overlay. EUV overlay, throughput and mask defects are not simulated either. Drawings you make are sent to EUV without mask correction; the ILT presets show what correction does.

      Why the stochastic model matters

      Photon shot noise is skewed, so the Gaussian tail is wrong

      At low dose, acid counts follow a compound Poisson distribution. A Gaussian with the same mean and variance gets the tails wrong, and in opposite directions. Bridges (resist that should stay dark prints) live in the upper tail, which the Gaussian under-predicts by up to 35× here. Breaks live in the lower tail, which it over-predicts. anamorph's saddlepoint tail uses the exact cumulant generating function and stays inside the Monte-Carlo confidence interval.

      What this page is and is not
      • The scanner and resist numbers are an illustrative High-NA card from public literature, not calibrated to any tool. Absolute P_fail values are model-relative.
      • The browser uses the 24 strongest of the engine's SOCS kernels (aerial error ≤ 0.0075 against the full 0.995-energy set). On load it checks itself against the engine at 48 pixels per clip.
      • Thin-mask optics only: 3D mask effects need a rigorous reference that is not built yet.
      • Source: github.com/aabhimittal/anamorph. Regenerate the data with python demo/build_data.py.
